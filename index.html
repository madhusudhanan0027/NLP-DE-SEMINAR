What Is Discourse?

Discourse refers to connected language that forms a meaningful communication. It can appear as a conversation, dialogue, paragraph, article, speech, or other connected form of communication.

A sentence gives information, but discourse provides connected meaning across multiple sentences or utterances.

Linguists study discourse to understand how speakers and writers link ideas together using reference, order, and connecting words — not just what each sentence says on its own, but how sentences relate to the ones around them.

Visual hierarchy

  1. WORD
  2. SENTENCE
  3. DISCOURSE UNIT

Example — a short discourse

“I went to college yesterday.

The seminar was interesting.

I learned about NLP.”

Three separate sentences — but read together, they form one connected discourse about a single event.

02

What Is Discourse Segmentation?

Discourse segmentation is the task of identifying boundaries that divide a text or conversation into meaningful discourse units.

  • Where one meaningful unit ends
  • Where the next unit begins
  • How connected pieces of language should be organized

I went to college yesterday. The seminar was amazing. I learned about NLP.

DISCOURSE UNIT 1

I went to college yesterday.

DISCOURSE UNIT 2

The seminar was amazing.

DISCOURSE UNIT 3

I learned about NLP.

Segmentation does not simply split text randomly. It attempts to identify meaningful boundaries.

03

Why Do We Need It?

NLP systems often need to understand how information is structured before performing higher-level language tasks.

Text Summarization

Identifies meaningful units that can be selected and combined into a summary.

Machine Translation

Helps systems preserve the organization and relationships present in the source text.

Question Answering

Helps identify relevant sections of a document or conversation.

Chatbots

Supports better understanding of conversational turns and topic changes.

Information Extraction

Helps organize information contained in connected text.

RAW TEXT → STRUCTURED UNITS → BETTER LANGUAGE PROCESSING

What Signals Help?

Segmentation systems can use multiple linguistic signals together — no single signal always determines a boundary on its own.

Punctuation

Periods, commas, question marks, and other punctuation can provide clues about boundaries.

Discourse Connectives

Words such as however, therefore, because, and so can indicate relationships between ideas.

Lexical Information

Words and vocabulary patterns can help identify changes or continuity in topics.

Syntactic Information

Sentence structure and grammatical relationships provide useful signals.

Semantic Information

Meaning and relationships between ideas help determine whether units belong together.

Context

Surrounding sentences or utterances can provide information needed to interpret a possible boundary.

MULTIPLE SIGNALS → BOUNDARY DECISION
06

Major Approaches

1

Rule-Based

Rule-based systems use predefined linguistic rules, punctuation patterns, cue phrases, and structural information to identify likely boundaries.

Advantages

  • Simple
  • Interpretable
  • Easy to design for specific patterns

Limitations

  • Depends heavily on hand-crafted rules
  • Can struggle with variation and ambiguity
2

Machine Learning

Machine learning approaches learn segmentation patterns from labeled examples and use linguistic features to classify likely discourse boundaries.

Advantages

  • Learns from data
  • More flexible than manually written rules

Limitations

  • Requires labeled training data
  • Performance depends on feature quality
3

Deep Learning

Deep learning approaches use neural networks to learn complex representations from language and context.

Conceptually includes: Recurrent Neural Networks · Transformers · Context-aware neural models

Advantages

  • Learns complex patterns
  • Can use broader context

Limitations

  • Requires substantial data and computation
  • Less interpretable than simple rules
RULE-BASED → MACHINE LEARNING → DEEP LEARNING

See It In Action

I wanted to attend the workshop. However, I had an exam. So I joined the recording later.

SEGMENT 1

I wanted to attend the workshop.

SEGMENT 2

However, I had an exam.

SEGMENT 3

Contrast

However indicates contrast — the exam works against the plan to attend.

Result

So can indicate a result or consequence that follows from what came before.

TEXT→ LINGUISTIC CUES→ BOUNDARY DETECTION→ SEGMENTS→ RELATIONSHIPS

A second example

I studied for the exam.

Therefore, I felt confident.

Applications & Challenges

Applications

  • Chatbots — tracking conversational turns and topic shifts.
  • Text Summarization — selecting units worth condensing.
  • Machine Translation — preserving structure across languages.
  • Question Answering — locating the relevant unit to answer from.
  • Search & Document Analysis — organizing long documents into retrievable pieces.
  • Dialogue Systems — following how a conversation's topic evolves.

Challenges

Ambiguous Boundaries

Some boundaries are difficult to identify because the same sentence can support different interpretations.

Context Dependence

Understanding a boundary may require information from surrounding text.

Informal Language

Chat and social media language can be noisy, incomplete, or irregular.

Long Documents

Relationships may span multiple sentences or paragraphs.

Different Domains

Language structure can vary across academic, conversational, technical, and other domains.

Spoken Language

Speech can contain pauses, repairs, interruptions, and prosodic cues not directly represented in written text.

REAL-WORLD LANGUAGE → AMBIGUITY → SEGMENTATION CHALLENGE
